There are many unique and interesting ways to use any type of photography. Photos have been around since the 1820s, so they have been around for 200 years now, which is crazy to think about. I remember when I was pretty young, I had this old Sony digital camera.
I used to mess around with it all the time and take random pictures of objects around my house or the park outside. Now those photos are long gone because the camera broke, and I never backed them up onto a hard drive or anything. So when I think about those photos, I don't think about the composition or how well lit it was; I think about the memories of taking those photos. Photos were designed to capture specific moments that could be taken as accurately as possible, to show how the human eye sees the world. As technology becomes more and more advanced, the quality of these photos becomes clearer.
Now, in this day and age, we have people constantly taking selfies of themselves or taking pictures of unique experiences every day. Photos like these are what is able to shape the current social media environment. I am kind of like that as well, as I like to take photos of myself and the world, too. However, I don't usually check out the contents of the photos. Yes, I would carefully take the picture, but I don't go back to look at it. At most, I would see any cool pictures I've taken and post them on my story on Instagram. I'm not really one to analyze my photos because I don't care about the photo itself.
I care about the memory of taking that photo, and thus, taking that photo helps me remember that moment in time. There is photography, but would I think about image making? Usually, I won't change photos I've already taken because I don't check back on those photos. However, I do like to participate in changing images made by other people. I think that image making is about changing other images to make them in your own style. Many people I know like to do edits to different images because they find something special about them. Even though they're not my images, the fact that they are available to the public means the photographer or artist wants people to interact with these images.
While there are many uses for photos, and I believe they can be used as a way to remember an event, the passage believes that photos can take us away from reality. Almost everything that is being used in technology nowadays is becoming more corporate with the excessive use of AI. AI images don't have any origin and are artificially made, which takes away from the authenticity of photos. There is a type of feeling of looking back on a photo from decades ago that makes you feel like you were in that specific time period. If AI is used heavily more heavily from now on, then years from now, people will remember the 2020s as the decade where photos became artificial, lacking soul.

‘AI slop’ distorting our reality, the world is sleepwalking into disaster

What is really defined as slop? Whenever I think of slop, I think of something that isn't good for you, but people still eat it up. Something like junk food is a good example. We all eat junk food but we know it isn't the most healthiest. And I would say that also applies to AI. We all have used AI at times even if we know how it can rot our brain. The problem with AI is the quality control problems, and how it focuses on quantity over quality.
For example, AI images are very easy to make, as all you need is a prompt to enter in chat gpt and the image comes out in less than a minute. There isn't any skill that is needed to create the images. But as AI develops, these images will be easier to create. Actual Art, made by humans, has been a thing for thousands of years now. Human Art has many different types of it and many different skill levels that are involved with it. And as art progressed over thousands of years, so has the quality of art. I would say that it is more rewarding to put out something you actually put in effort to make, rather than generate something very quickly and easily for making a quick buck or post on social media. In a perfect world, we would prefer the more quality approach of art, but not every one can put in the time to create art or learn how to create it.
In the article it says "But whatever the intent of its creators, this torrent of AI content leads to the desensitisation and overwhelming of visual palates. The overall effect of being exposed to AI images all the time, from the nonsensical to the soothing to the ideological, is that everything begins to land in a different way." I think what the author means by this is the more AI art is abused and overused, the less appreciation people would have for human art. Now people can be easily tricked by AI content farms that are disguised and regular content. Whenever I'm on social media, I consistently see low effort videos that is of a certain topic being voice narrated by an AI, with a clearly written AI script, but they will still pull in thousands or millions of views. Another point the article makes that resonated with me is "The result is that all media consumption, even for the most discerning users, becomes impossible to curate. You are immersed deeper and deeper into subjective worlds rather than objective reality." This means that as we get more and more consumed by AI, the actual and human parts of the world will be desensitized and look less appealing. This to me speaks to how the world is slowly becoming more and more dystopian.
This is a real shame and I think to combat this, we need to show more appreciation of the neccasities that we already have. I think this is a lesson to be thankful of what we know is real and care for. In a blink of an eye, that could all be gone and replaced by tools without a soul.
